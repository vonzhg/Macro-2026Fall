Assignments

What to hand in, every time. One PDF with your answers and figures, the code that produced them, and an AI_LOG.md recording how you used AI assistance. Every number you report must come from code you ran.

Homework 1 — From a question to a quantitative answer Available Discuss

15% of the grade. Part A out October 8, Part B out October 15; both due Thursday, October 29. A small research project on Sessions 1–5, in which you are the project manager: one model, a positive question (how much of U.S. business-cycle volatility do TFP shocks explain?) and a normative one (what do aggregate fluctuations cost in welfare?), taken from the charter to the paper.

Part A — Scope, Model, Validation, Data Download PDF

A one-page project charter; the planner problem, the recursive competitive equilibrium and what a representative agent cannot say; a unit test of your solver against the closed-form growth model; and your own data targets and calibration from FRED, with an AI-assisted provenance table you verify by hand.

Part B — Method, Answer, Presentation Download PDF

Choose the algorithm on the evidence; solve the calibrated model by a global method, first-order perturbation and Chebyshev collocation (Session 5 parts after October 22); answer both questions; and deliver a six-page paper, five slides, a one-command replication package, and an AI_LOG.md with a validation ledger. Question B2(d) feeds the anonymous class accuracy leaderboard. You also choose one route to go deeper — R1 the numerical frontier, R2 data and estimation, or R3 capital-income taxation — and end by pitching your own question on the forum's Pitches and teams board. Part A includes an AI red-team item: find and document an error in an AI assistant's derivation.

Templates — charter, AI log, run_all.py, paper, slides charter.md AI_LOG.md run_all.py paper.tex slides.tex
The leaderboard ranks accuracy reported with its cost, not accuracy alone. Submit your maximum log₁₀ℰ together with the grid size, the tolerance, and the wall time.

Homework 2 — Heterogeneity and learning Available Discuss

15%. Part A out October 29, Part B out November 12; due Monday, November 23. Sessions 6–9. A common core — the Aiyagari economy as a nested fixed point, a neural-network solution validated against a classical one, a first reinforcement-learning exercise, and a first text-as-data measurement — plus one route of your choice: aggregate risk and the welfare cost of cycles with heterogeneity, fiscal policy and transitions, AI solution methods, or AI and inequality. You also audit a classmate's Homework 1 replication package, and red-team an AI assistant once more.

Final project Guidelines posted Discuss

40%, in teams of 2–3. The project assembles what the homework built: combine at least one Homework 1 component and one Homework 2 component from your team, validate each against its homework benchmark, and add what your team's question still needs — a model, a data source, or a method — with one classical and one AI/ML method cross-validated on the same object.
