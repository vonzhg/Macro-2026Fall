Modern macroeconomics asks questions about distributions and policy: who bears income risk, how inequality interacts with the business cycle, how aging and public debt burden different cohorts, what a government should tax when it cannot commit, and how artificial intelligence will reshape work and wages. Answering these questions requires models with heterogeneous agents, frictions, and strategic governments — and those models can almost never be solved with pencil and paper.

The course builds the foundations in three steps. On paper (Sessions 1–2) we go from the neoclassical growth model to the workhorse models of current research — Bewley–Huggett–Aiyagari, Krusell–Smith, overlapping generations, and optimal fiscal policy with and without commitment. On the machine (Sessions 3–6) we learn to solve them: numerical dynamic programming, Python and PyTorch, root-finding, optimization, differentiation, approximation, quadrature, discretizing stochastic processes, perturbation and projection — ending at the computational wall that heterogeneous-agent economies run into. At the frontier (Sessions 7–10) we study how machine learning, deep learning and reinforcement learning break through that wall, how language models turn text into economic data, and how AI agents are changing the way research is done.

Throughout, the course insists on one discipline: every computed answer must be benchmarked and verified. As AI makes generating answers cheap, verification becomes the scarce skill. Every number on a slide in this course was produced by code that is in this repository, and every accuracy claim is reported with its cost.

The companion course. Where this course ends, AI for Economic Research: Dynamic Models, Language, and Agents begins — ten lectures on deep learning for dynamic models, reinforcement learning with heterogeneous agents, LLMs and text as economic data, retrieval-augmented generation, and agentic AI research workflows. Sessions 7–10 here are a compressed tour of that material; the companion site is where it is developed in full, with its own labs.

Start here

Three steps per session

  1. Read the session row in the syllabus — it says what each block covers and what is due.
  2. Work through the decks on the slides page. Each block is three or four short decks rather than one long one.
  3. Run the lab. The notebooks are where the material becomes yours. They ship with no stored output on purpose — you are meant to produce the numbers.
No Python yet? Start with Lab 0, posted before Session 3. It installs the tools and checks the install. Programming is taught from the ground up in Session 3 — prior experience helps but is not assumed.

Where this course sits

This site is the foundations stage: the models, and the classical methods that solve them. Its continuation is AI for Economic Research, which takes the same models to the AI frontier — deep learning for dynamic models, reinforcement learning and heterogeneous agents, language models and text as data, and agentic research workflows.
